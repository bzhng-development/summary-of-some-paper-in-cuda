Here are the takeaways from this Ryan Peterman article (on why most engineers aren't "10x engineers"):

**The two missing ingredients for 10x impact**

Peterman boils the "10x engineer" concept down to two necessary components, arguing that most engineers lack both: **domain expertise** and **influence**. Domain expertise means a deep understanding of a specific area that lets an engineer solve problems few others can. Influence means the ability to lead major initiatives and guide large groups of engineers to build what matters most—impact that goes beyond individual code contributions. 

**Permissioned vs. permissionless paths**

He draws a sharp distinction between how you acquire these two capabilities. Leading large initiatives through influence is **"permissioned"** — someone has to give you that scope, and everyone is chasing those positions. You earn them by building a track record of trust over time. Domain expertise, by contrast, is **"permissionless"** and therefore fully in your control. It's hard work, but no one can stop you from pursuing it. 

**Curiosity as the engine of expertise**

The best way to develop domain expertise, Peterman argues, is through curiosity. The strongest engineers he knows chase problems further than anyone else because they're genuinely curious to understand the root cause. The advice is straightforward: "Let your curiosity guide your learning." It's one of the most reliable ways to build the kind of expertise that lets you solve problems few others can.
