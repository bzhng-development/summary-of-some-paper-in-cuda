## 1. Executive Summary

This paper studies how to optimally allocate test-time computation for LLMs on math reasoning, analyzing two mechanisms—searching against dense, process-based verifier reward models (e.g., beam search vs. best-of-N) and updating the model’s distribution over a response adaptively (e.g., sequential self-revision vs. parallel sampling)—on the MATH benchmark using PaLM 2‑S\*. A prompt-conditioned compute-optimal strategy improves efficiency by more than 4× (matching best-of-N accuracy with 4× fewer generations) and in a FLOPs-matched comparison lets a smaller model outperform a ∼14× larger pretrained model on easy-to-medium questions, establishing that test-time compute substitutes for pretraining only when problems lie within the base model’s capability range.
