Here are the takeaways from this Ryan Peterman letter on job hunting in the layoff era (market analysis and a startup-evaluation framework):

**The FAANG hiring landscape has contracted to senior backfill**

Ryan opens by acknowledging the wave of messages he's received from people considering leaving their companies, against a backdrop of layoffs at every FAANG-like company except Apple. The hiring that remains at these large firms is "limited to senior IC roles and a small amount for backfilling attrition." The broader dynamic is simple: fewer roles, more competition.

**High-growth startups are still growing at breakneck pace**

Despite the doom-and-gloom narrative around big tech, Ryan notes that "there are still some smaller companies growing at a breakneck pace." He compiled a public Google Sheet of example startups with headcount growth and fundraising rounds, finding companies growing "like rocketships" in the last six months—OpenAI at +45%, Notion at +60%, Figma at +101%. Some have even raised fresh funding in the current down environment (Anthropic, Adept AI, OpenAI), signaling "a strong enough narrative and metrics to support growth still." He also pulled median compensation from levels.fyi for several examples, noting that these high-growth companies offer total comp "comparable to what you'd expect at FAANG."

**Public vs. startup job searches demand different homework**

Ryan draws a sharp contrast: public-company equity is priced daily by the market—"if a recruiter says your offer contains $100k of RSUs, you'll receive that amount barring market fluctuations"—and you don't need to question the company's survival. Startups require "much more homework." He lays out a three-signal framework for evaluating them.

**Signal 1: Fundraising history and valuation accuracy**

A company that fundraised in the past six months demonstrates growth potential even in a down market. Fresh funding extends the startup's runway, which "helps with job security"—some observers are even arguing these startups are *more* stable than FAANG-like companies right now. Recent fundraising also means "the company's valuation is more likely accurate." He warns against joining a company that "raised an overvalued round in 2021 but is worth much less in reality now," which would leave new hires holding equity priced far above true value.

**Signal 2: Headcount growth as a proxy for health and opportunity**

Since private companies don't disclose headcount, Ryan used LinkedIn Premium insights to approximate it ("pretty close"). He offers his public spreadsheet as a free alternative for those who don't want to pay. Headcount growth is not only "a measure of opportunity for career growth" but also "one of the top signs a startup is doing well and will raise at a higher valuation later"—though he cautions to "take it with a grain of salt though since anything can happen in startups."

**Signal 3: Company stage must match risk appetite**

He references his earlier writing on compensation differences between startups and big tech: "smaller companies have less liquid, more volatile equity." The practical test is whether "the company's stage matches your risk appetite" and whether "the cash portion of the compensation is enough for you in the worst case"—a framing that treats equity as a potential bonus rather than a line item in a living-expenses budget.
