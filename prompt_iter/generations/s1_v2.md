## 1. Executive Summary

The paper studies how to optimally allocate test-time computation for LLMs on the MATH benchmark using PaLM 2‑S*, analyzing two primary mechanisms: (1) searching against a process‑based reward model (PRM) verifier (e.g., beam search vs. best‑of‑N) and (2) iterative revisions that update the model’s proposal distribution (e.g., sequential revisions vs. parallel sampling). The core contribution is a **compute‑optimal scaling strategy** that adaptively selects the allocation per prompt based on its estimated difficulty, yielding more than 4× better efficiency than a best‑of‑N baseline. In a FLOPs‑matched comparison, the smaller model with compute‑optimal test‑time compute can outperform a ∼14× larger pretrained model on easy‑to‑medium questions, but “on challenging questions which are outside a given base model’s capabilities … pretraining is likely more effective” (Section 7).
