## 1. Executive Summary

This paper analyzes how to optimally allocate a fixed test-time compute budget for LLMs on the MATH benchmark using PaLM 2-S*, studying two primary mechanisms — searching against process-based reward model (PRM) verifiers (e.g., beam search vs. best-of-N) and iteratively revising the model’s own responses to modify the proposal distribution (e.g., sequential revision chains vs. parallel sampling) — and finds that the efficacy of these strategies depends critically on prompt difficulty. The core contribution is a **compute-optimal test-time scaling strategy** that adaptively selects search algorithms, revision depth, and parallel-vs-sequential sampling ratios per difficulty bin, yielding **more than 4× higher efficiency** over a best-of-N baseline (e.g., matching 256-generation performance with only 64 generations) and demonstrating, in a FLOPs-matched comparison, that a smaller model with compute-optimal test-time compute can outperform a **~14× larger** pretrained model on easy-to-medium problems. However, the approach provides negligible gains on the hardest problems (difficulty level 5), and test-time compute is not 1-to-1 exchangeable with pretraining — the benefit is bounded by the base model’s existing capability — while the practical cost of the difficulty estimation itself (2048 samples per question) is not accounted for.
