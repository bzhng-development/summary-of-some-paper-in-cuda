Here are the takeaways from this Ryan Peterman article on avoiding down-leveling when interviewing for FAANG roles:

**Titles are useful but context-dependent**

Titles give us a sense of the scope an engineer can handle—knowing someone is a Senior engineer tells you they can manage more ambiguity, and titles are a concise way to market your abilities. But titles only mean something within the context of the company they come from. Some companies have significant title inflation that devalues their titles; engineers at banks can get the title of "Vice President" with just a few years of experience. You can use levels.fyi to compare levels across well-known companies. The real risk of down-leveling comes when you're at a company that isn't as well known, like startups or places outside Silicon Valley.

**Scope is king**

If your company and title aren't well-known, FAANG recruiters make an educated guess about what level to interview you for, based on your years of experience and the scope of your work. If you have plenty of years of experience, the scope of your work is what most influences their guess. They'll compare it to the expectations of FAANG levels—Mid-level (IC4), Senior (IC5), Staff (IC6), and Senior Staff (IC7). You can compare your own scope against those expectations to see where you fit. You might find the work you're doing matches a level with a less senior title than you currently hold; in that case, it's not really a "down level"—it's just a title change because companies have different naming conventions.

If you want to interview for higher levels at FAANG companies, focus on growing the scope of your work at your current company, then make sure your resume communicates that larger scope. Be clear with the recruiter on the level you're interviewing for before taking interviews. After that, it's in your hands to interview well enough to retain that level—though many engineers get down-leveled during system design interviews, which is a topic for another discussion.

---

Ryan's central framing—that **scope is king** and titles are merely context-dependent shorthand—mirrors a recurring theme across career-growth writing: external markers matter less than the work itself. His advice to compare your actual scope against FAANG level expectations, then calibrate your resume and recruiter conversations accordingly, is a practical application of the idea that interviewing leveling is an *educated guess* based on observable scope, not a verdict on your worth. The "it's not a down-level if the scope matches" reframe underlines his broader point: chasing titles without the underlying scope is hollow, but growing scope naturally pulls the title along.
