## 1. Executive Summary

This paper studies how to optimally allocate a fixed budget of inference-time computation for LLMs on challenging math reasoning problems—using PaLM 2-S\* on the MATH benchmark—by analyzing two primary mechanisms: searching against process-based verifier reward models (e.g., beam search vs. best-of-N) and adaptively modifying the model’s output distribution through iterative self-revisions (e.g., sequential revision chains vs. parallel sampling). A compute-optimal strategy that selects strategies per prompt based on estimated difficulty improves test-time compute efficiency by more than \(4\times\) (matching a 256-generation baseline with only 64 generations) and enables a smaller model with compute-optimal scaling to outperform a \(\sim 14\times\) larger pretrained model in a FLOPs-matched comparison, establishing that these gains are bounded by prompt difficulty—on the hardest questions, no test-time strategy helps, and scaling pretraining remains necessary.
