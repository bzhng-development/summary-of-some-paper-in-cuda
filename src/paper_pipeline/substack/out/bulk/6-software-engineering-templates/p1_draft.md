Here are the takeaways from this Ryan Peterman newsletter entry (a curated collection of software engineering writing templates):

**The premise: writing as a repeatable, structural engineering skill**

Ryan frames writing as one of the most important skills for software engineers, but also one that is highly repetitive in structure. Design docs, postmortems, diff summaries—each has a predictable shape. The insight is that you don’t need to reinvent the scaffold every time; you can save time and mental energy by starting from a proven template. The post is presented as a living reference page to bookmark and copy from whenever one of these common use cases arises.

**Design Doc template**

The design doc is positioned as a tool for *proposing* future engineering work and persisting details for discussion, not just specifying them. Ryan’s personal template is offered as a starting structure for that proposal-and-feedback loop.

**Postmortem Review template**

When systems break, thinking through how to do better next time is a necessity. Ryan links to a separate piece on postmortem fundamentals and provides his personal postmortem review template to structure both the investigation and the discussion that follows.

**Diff (or PR) Summary template**

After writing hundreds of diffs, Ryan noticed that all their descriptions share a similar structure. His personal diff summary template is designed to be the starting point for *every* code change he makes—a uniform framing that saves time and ensures consistency across an entire body of patches.

**Direction Doc template**

For engineers in leadership roles who have to set team direction, Ryan offers a personal direction doc template that he uses for any strategic initiative. It is designed to be malleable across varied settings: major engineering initiatives, team strategy docs, partnership alignment discussions. The template provides a common spine that can be bent to fit the context.

**Eng Project Management (timeline/Gantt chart)**

For larger initiatives with many moving parts, having a project timeline is often helpful. Ryan shares a simple timeline spreadsheet (a Gantt-chart structure) that he always starts from, with a note that you may want to add additional columns depending on the project's complexity.

**Launch Post template**

Writing that announces results and shares them with a broader audience almost always follows the same structure. Ryan’s launch post template captures that structure, saving time when the work is done and the story needs to be told.

**The meta-pattern: treat templates as living tools**

The post itself is designed as an evolving artifact—Ryan explicitly commits to keeping the page updated so it always contains the best software engineering templates, and he includes a feedback form inviting readers to suggest additions. The templates aren't presented as rigid prescriptions but as personal defaults to copy, edit, and mold. The underlying philosophy is that **good writing structure can be pre-solved**, freeing engineers to focus on the substance of what they're actually communicating rather than on how to arrange it.
