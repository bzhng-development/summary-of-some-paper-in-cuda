Here are the takeaways from this Ryan Peterman article (comparing the Staff (L6) and Senior Staff (L7) levels in the FAANG career ladder):

**Similar Behaviors, Larger Scope**

The core premise is that the behaviors required for L6 and L7 are fundamentally similar, but there is a "truth in the rationale" behind the observation that L7 promotions can seem easier—the path is already familiar. Both levels are expected to lead projects and shape engineering culture beyond their immediate team. The critical differentiator is scope of influence: an L6 influences across teams of roughly 8 or more engineers, while an L7 operates across broader engineering organizations of 50 or more. Both levels are expected to solve problems other engineers cannot, with the L7 being an "even stronger engineer" who solves problems an L6 would struggle with. Both levels grow other engineers, but L7s do so with higher leverage by growing L6s, who "already get a lot done." This broader influence is what unlocks the significantly greater impact of an L7; they work in a similar way to an L6, just with much more leverage.

**Engineering Archetypes**

When Ryan first joined Facebook as an L3, stories about L7+ "engineering archetypes" inspired him, making engineers at these levels "sound like superheroes." These archetypes are high-level descriptions of how engineers at senior levels have impact, serving as tools for managers to discuss the otherwise ambiguous expectations of IC7+ engineers. An engineer can fit one or more archetypes and may switch between them depending on their projects. Knowing them helps clarify expectations. While there are general industry classifications, Ryan finds clear gaps in some—citing missing "code machine" and "specialist" archetypes—and much prefers Meta's internal classification. The same pattern of similar behaviors but larger scope that distinguishes L6 from L7 extends upward to define the expectations for IC8+ engineers. Big tech is noted as one of the few environments that allows for significant career growth without switching to management, as smaller companies simply don't have as much business need for IC7+ engineers.
