## 1. Executive Summary

This paper studies how to optimally allocate a fixed test-time compute budget for large language models on the MATH benchmark using PaLM 2-S*, analyzing two primary mechanisms: searching against a process reward model (PRM) verifier (beam search vs. best-of-N) and refining the model’s proposal distribution through iterative revisions (sequential revisions vs. parallel sampling). The central insight is a **compute-optimal test-time scaling strategy** that selects search and revision hyperparameters per prompt based on an estimated difficulty statistic, yielding more than 4× efficiency gains over a best-of-N baseline — for PRM search, a budget of 16 generations nearly matches the accuracy of 64 generations with best-of-N, and for revisions, 64 generations match 256. A FLOPs-matched comparison establishes that the smaller model with compute-optimal test-time compute can outperform a ~14× larger pretrained model on easy-to-medium problems, but the gains vanish for the hardest questions where the base model’s pass@1 is near zero, demonstrating that test-time compute amplifies existing capability rather than creating it.
